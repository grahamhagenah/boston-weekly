DECODING:ENCODING | Sara Bronin

MIT · Wed, Sep 16 · 12:30pm

The Leventhal Center for Advanced Urbanism, co-sponsored by the joint program in City Design and Development (CDD) and the Housing Community and Economic Development (HCED) group is thrilled to invite you to DECODING:ENCODING. Our fall lecture series explores the reciprocal relationship between the rules that shape urban environments and the technologies that help us understand them. We are asking how social, political, economic, and cultural values become embedded in urban systems and how contemporary computational tools can help us uncover, critique, and reimagine those embedded assumptions.

Time: 12:30pm - 2pm · Location: W41-1306

This week we welcome Sara Bronin, Freda H. Alverson Professor of Law George Washington University Law School. Sara C. Bronin is a Mexican-American architect, attorney, George Washington University professor, and changemaker whose interdisciplinary work focuses on how law and policy can foster more equitable, sustainable, well-designed, and connected places. She wrote Key to the City: How Zoning Shapes Our World, and she founded and leads the National Zoning Atlas and National Preservation Atlas, which illuminate land use rules influencing our economy, society, health, and happiness. She also served in a Presidentially-appointed, Senate-confirmed role chairing the federal historic preservation agency.
